Professor profiles

Michael T. Cahill
Professor of Law · President & Dean Emeritus (2019–23)
CRIMINAL LAW · CRM-100-D1
Education
J.D. magna cum laude & M.P.P., University of Michigan (note editor, Michigan Law Review); B.A., Yale. Clerked for Judge James B. Loken, 8th Circuit.
Path to BLS
13 years on faculty (2003–16) → Co-Dean, Rutgers Law (2016–19) → President & Dean, Brooklyn Law (2019–23) → returned to full-time teaching in 2023.
Scholarship
Criminal law theory (desert/retributive justice), criminal code drafting, sentencing policy, criminal juries. Consulted on criminal-code reform in Illinois and Kentucky.
Key works
Law Without Justice (Oxford, 2006, w/ Paul H. Robinson); Criminal Law treatise (Aspen, 2nd ed. 2012); Criminal Law: Case Studies and Controversies casebook (Wolters Kluwer, 4th ed. 2016, w/ Shima Baradaran Baughman).
Reviews
RMP page exists but is blocked from scraping. One third-party aggregator lists 4.6/5 with the note "strong leadership in criminal law" — that phrasing reads like a dean-bio blurb, not classroom feedback, so treat it as low-confidence at best.
What this means for you His own casebook pairs black-letter doctrine with real-world "controversies" — if it's assigned, expect class to pivot from "what's the rule" to "should the law be this way." His retributive-justice lens means recurring "why do we punish" questions. His code-drafting background makes the Model Penal Code essential — get the MPC excerpts before day one.
Brian A. Lee
Professor of Law · ALI Member · Assoc. Reporter, Restatement (Fourth) of Property
PROPERTY · PTE-100-D2
Education
Ph.D. Philosophy, Princeton (1999, dissertation on moral obligations to past generations); J.D., Yale (2008, Yale Law Journal editor); undergrad Classics, Berkeley.
At BLS
Faculty since 2015. Also teaches Remedies, Intellectual Property, and International Law.
Scholarship
Takings/eminent domain, philosophy of property, compensation mechanisms and relational justice. Published in Columbia, Texas, and Michigan law reviews.
Key works
"Uncompensated Takings: Insurance, Efficiency, and Relational Justice"; "Coasean Property" (w/ Henry E. Smith).
Reviews
RMP page exists but is blocked from scraping, and no forum discussion turned up. The one concrete artifact found: his Spring 2019 Property final (via a student-uploaded outline) was open-book and essay-based — multiple substantive questions plus one complex multi-part fact pattern, with an explicit instruction not to over-invest time in any single question. Useful pacing signal even without a "review" attached.
What this means for you A philosopher-lawyer — expect doctrine taught through theory (Locke's labor theory, Demsetz's economic account, Blackstone's "bundle of sticks") rather than pure rule memorization. Given his takings specialty, don't be surprised if regulatory takings gets outsized attention even at the 1L level.
Cristina Carmody Tilley
Professor & Claire Ferguson-Carlson Faculty Fellow, University of Iowa College of Law
TORTS · TRT-100-D2S
Education
J.D. cum laude, Northwestern (Order of the Coif, Lowden-Wigmore Prize).
Current post
Primary public appointment is still University of Iowa; also a 2024–25 Visiting Fellow at Princeton's Center for Human Values. Her CV doesn't yet list Brooklyn Law — this looks like a new or visiting appointment for Fall 2026, worth a quick confirm once on campus.
Scholarship
Tort theory & social justice, dignitary torts, defamation, First Amendment/tort intersections, private law & policing/technology. Already published in the Brooklyn Law Review ("A New Private Law of Policing," 2024).
Key works
Co-author, Cases and Materials on the Law of Torts (7th ed.); "Tort Law Inside Out" (Yale L.J., 2017); "(Re)categorizing Defamation" (Tulane L. Rev., 2020) — specifically on the history of NYT v. Sullivan.
Reviews
RMP page exists under her Iowa listing but is blocked from scraping. No substantive review text surfaced anywhere, and since this appears to be her first BLS semester, even her Iowa reputation may not transfer directly — the classroom experience here is genuinely unknown territory.
What this means for you Given her published critique of NYT v. Sullivan, expect defamation and dignitary torts to get real weight, not treated as a con-law afterthought. Her "law and social justice" framing means expect discussion of who tort law protects — and who it doesn't.

Class-by-class reading prep

Criminal Law
CRM-100-D1 · Cahill · Mon/Wed 10:00–11:55 · Rm 501
Act & mental state
Martin v. State— the voluntary act requirement
Robinson v. California— can't criminalize status alone
Regina v. Cunningham— recklessness
Morissette v. United States— courts read intent into silent statutes
Homicide
Commonwealth v. Carroll— premeditation
State v. Guthrie— adequate provocation / heat of passion
People v. Stamp— felony murder
Causation & defenses
Regina v. Dudley and Stephens— necessity
People v. Goetz— subjective vs. objective self-defense (NY case — likely emphasized)
State v. Norman— imperfect self-defense
Inchoate crimes
People v. Rizzo— attempt / dangerous proximity
Pinkerton v. United States— conspiracy liability
Prep move Read MPC §2.02 (the purposely/knowingly/recklessly/negligently ladder) before week one — Cahill's whole scholarly output is built around MPC structure, and this section underlies almost everything else in the course.
Property
PTE-100-D2 · Lee · Mon/Wed 1:50–3:45 · Rm 603
First possession & found property
Pierson v. Post— wild animals, capture vs. pursuit
Ghen v. Rich— custom as law (whaling)
Popov v. Hayashi— qualified possession (the baseball case)
Armory v. Delamirie— finders' rights
Estates, adverse possession, landlord-tenant
White v. Brown— fee simple vs. life estate interpretation
Van Valkenburgh v. Lutz— adverse possession elements
Javins v. First National Realty— implied warranty of habitability
Land use & takings
Boomer v. Atlantic Cement Co.— nuisance, damages vs. injunction
Penn Central Transportation Co. v. NYC— regulatory takings test
Kelo v. City of New London— public use / eminent domain
Prep move Property is vocabulary-heavy before it's rule-heavy. Learn the estates & future interests system (fee simple, life estate, remainder, reversion) cold before diving into cases, or the first few weeks read as noise. Given Lee's takings specialty, don't skim Penn Central / Kelo even if your syllabus treats them as a brief unit — he may linger there.
Torts
TRT-100-D2S · Tilley · Tue/Thu 1:50–3:45 · Rm 503
Intentional torts (you've already drilled these)
Vosburg v. Putney— unlawful-act doctrine
Garratt v. Dailey— transferred/constructive intent
Negligence & duty
Palsgraf v. Long Island R.R.— Cardozo vs. Andrews on scope of duty
Tarasoff v. Regents of Univ. of California— duty to warn third parties
United States v. Carroll Towing— Learned Hand's BPL formula
Byrne v. Boadle— res ipsa loquitur
Causation, strict liability, products
Eggshell plaintiff rule— you've already got this doctrine covered
Rylands v. Fletcher— strict liability origins
MacPherson v. Buick— privity collapses in products liability
Defamation & dignitary torts — likely emphasized
New York Times v. Sullivan— actual-malice standard; Tilley has published specifically reframing this case's history
Gertz v. Robert Welch, Inc.— private vs. public figure defamation standards
Prep move Given Tilley's own published work on NYT v. Sullivan, read that case closely rather than treating it as a con-law crossover to skim. Keep building on your existing Cardozo/Andrews and Vosburg work — that foundation carries straight into her negligence unit. (You've already got Vosburg and Palsgraf read on this site.)

General things to look into

Confirm the real syllabi. Everything above is informed prediction from each professor's scholarship plus the standard 1L curriculum — not a confirmed reading list. Swap in whatever Canvas actually posts.
Start a rolling outline per class from week one. Don't wait until finals — outlining as you go is what actually works for issue-spotting exams.
Learn cold-call / Socratic-method norms. Know how to state a case's facts and holding cold, out loud, without notes.
Watch for NY-specific doctrine. BLS is in New York, and Cahill in particular is likely to bring in NY Penal Law and NY cases like People v. Goetz alongside the majority-rule cases.
Get the Model Penal Code excerpts now. It's foundational for Criminal Law and cheap/free in excerpt form (Foundation Press) — no reason to wait.
